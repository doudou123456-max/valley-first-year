Just Bought Stardew Valley in the Steam Sale? Your Calm First-Week Plan

Updated: September 26, 2026 · Unofficial fan guide · For Stardew Valley 1.6.15

The Steam Autumn Sale always brings a wave of brand-new farmers. Welcome. Here's everything that actually matters in your first week — on one screen, with no wiki rabbit hole and no FOMO.

Quick answers (read this first)

  1. You cannot fall behind in Stardew Valley — there is no timer, no score, no game over for a slow season.
  2. Day 1 has one job: plant and water your 15 starter parsnips. That is a successful first day.
  3. Watch TV for tomorrow's weather every night; rain waters your crops for free.
  4. Your first big purchase is the 24-slot backpack — before animals, decor, or anything fancy.
  5. It is completely safe to skip the Traveling Cart, profit tables, sprinkler math, and hard bundles in Year 1.

The only rule for Week 1

The single most common first-week mistake isn't anything you do in the game. It's opening the wiki, seeing hundreds of pages, and deciding you're already doing it wrong. You're not. Year 1 is a tutorial you're allowed to take slowly. Nobody is grading your farm, and every season loops around again.

Days 3–5: learn your two early skills in short trips

Fishing is your best zero-investment early money. Expect to be bad at the green-bar minigame for the first in-game day — everyone is. Fishing basics for Year 1.

The mines unlock around Day 5. Treat the first trips as scouts: short runs, always bring food, turn back while you're still healthy. Most new players faint on floor 5 because they pushed for one more rock. Early mines without passing out.

Energy is the mechanic behind 90% of first-week frustration. "Tired" is a suggestion to head home; the 2 AM collapse is the tax you pay for ignoring it. Stop passing out: an energy plan.

Rainy days are gifts, not bonus chaos

When it rains, outdoor crops are watered automatically — don't water them by habit out of spare energy. Pick one activity for the day: the mines, fishing, or chopping. Still pack food, still come home with energy left. Here's the full rainy-day decision plan.

Your daily loop (screenshot this)

  1. Read your mail.
  2. Watch TV for tomorrow's weather.
  3. Water crops and pet any animals.
  4. Ship everything in the bin before bed.
  5. Pick one afternoon goal — not five.
  6. Be home well before 2 AM.

What's safe to skip your first season

  • Crop profit tables and spreadsheet farming — parsnips, then whatever the season gives you, is fine.
  • Hard Community Center bundles. Save uncertain items in one chest and move on: a calm Community Center room order.
  • Sprinkler math, optimal layouts, and "perfect" farm tours online.
  • Comparing your Day 12 farm to someone's 100-day farm.
  • Worrying about updates. Version 1.6.15 is a complete, stable game; the announced 1.7 update has no release date, and nothing in Year 1 depends on it.

FAQ

Did I already ruin my save by making a "wrong" choice on Day 1?

No. There are no permanent wrong choices in the first week — spent energy regrows, missed forage respawns, and every season and year repeats. Even tool upgrades and crop choices are fully reversible over time.

Should I wait for the 1.7 update before playing?

No. 1.7 has no release date, and 1.6.15 is a huge, complete, stable game. Nothing you do in Year 1 will be wasted — saves carry forward through every free update, as they always have.

Is Stardew Valley playable on Steam Deck / controller?

Yes — it has full controller support and a Deck-verified build. This guide is platform-neutral; the only platform difference is when each platform receives patches, and 1.6 is out everywhere now.

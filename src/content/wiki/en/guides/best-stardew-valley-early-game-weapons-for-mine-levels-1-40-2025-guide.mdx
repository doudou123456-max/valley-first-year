# Best Stardew Valley Early-Game Weapons for Mine Levels 1-40 (2025 Guide)

If you’ve spent your first few in-game days clearing weeds, planting parsnips, and fixing up your farm, you’ve probably gotten the letter from the Adventurer’s Guild reminding you of the old mines north of Pelican Town. Those mines hold the copper, iron, and coal you need to upgrade your tools, craft sprinklers, and build better farm buildings—but to get those resources, you’ll have to fight off slimes, bats, and stone golems first. Picking the best Stardew Valley early game weapons for mines 1-40 can cut your mine run time in half, reduce the number of health potions you burn through, and help you hit the critical iron ore floors before your first month ends. Even if you have zero combat skill points invested, the right weapon will make clearing floors feel less like a grind and more like a fast path to farm upgrades.

## How to Unlock Weapon Access in Early Stardew Valley (First 3 In-Game Weeks)
Before you can start testing different weapons, you’ll need to unlock the places that sell or reward them. You don’t need to grind combat levels to get access to solid gear in your first few weeks—just follow these simple steps:
1. **Trigger the mine entrance cutscene**: On or after Day 5 of Spring, head north of Pelican Town to the mountain area. The Joja Corporation crew will be clearing the rockslide blocking the mine entrance, and the guild leader Marlon will give you your first free weapon: the rusty sword. This basic 2-5 damage weapon will get you through the first 10 floors, but you’ll want an upgrade fast.
2. **Unlock the Adventurer’s Guild**: Once you reach level 5 in the mines, Marlon will greet you again and invite you to the guild, located right next to the mine entrance. The guild sells low-tier weapons, slingshots, and combat supplies from the moment you unlock it, no combat level requirements.
3. **Check the Traveling Cart every Friday and Sunday**: The cart that spawns in the Cindersap Forest south of your farm sometimes sells rare early-game weapons for below guild prices, even if you have no combat progress. It’s always worth a quick stop before you head to the mines for the day.
4. **Open all chests in mine floors 1-30**: Most early-game weapon drops come from free chests in the mine, no gold or combat required. Even common chests can have gear that’s 2-3 times stronger than your starting rusty sword.

If you’re playing a new save in 2025, these unlock rules haven’t changed in the latest Stardew Valley update, so you can follow this timeline no matter if you’re playing on PC, console, or mobile. ## Tier List of Early-Game Weapons for Mine Levels 1-40 (Ranked by Damage & Ease of Acquisition)
We’ve ranked every weapon you can get before reaching mine level 40 based on total damage, attack speed, how easy it is to get, and how well it performs against the common enemies in the first 40 floors (slimes, green bugs, rock crabs, and dust sprites). This tier list prioritizes options that work even for players looking for the cheapest early game weapons Stardew Valley no combat skills required, so you don’t need to have leveled up your combat skill to use any of these.

### S-Tier: Best Overall Weapons for Floors 1-40
These weapons deal enough damage to one-shot most common enemies in the first 40 floors, and you can get them for free or for less than 500g before you even hit level 20 in the mines.
- **Steel Smallsword**: 6-11 damage, +2 defense, average attack speed. This is the undisputed best stardew valley early game weapon to beat mine floors 1-40, and it’s 100% free if you know where to find it. The extra defense point cuts damage from slimes and bats by 20% on its own, and its fast attack speed lets you stun-lock rock crabs before they can hide in their shells.
- **Wooden Club**: 8-16 damage, slow attack speed, +1 knockback. Available for 250g at the Adventurer’s Guild the second you unlock it, this club is perfect for players who prefer slower, heavier hits. The high knockback lets you keep crowds of slimes away from you, and its 16 max damage can one-shot green slimes before they get close enough to deal damage. It’s the best cheap option if you don’t want to hunt for the Steel Smallsword.

### A-Tier: Solid Alternatives if You Can’t Get S-Tier Gear
These weapons are slightly less powerful or harder to find than S-Tier options, but they’re still a massive upgrade from the starting rusty sword.
- **Silver Saber**: 7-13 damage, fast attack speed. Found in chests on mine levels 10-25, or sold for 500g at the Adventurer’s Guild once you hit combat level 1. Its fast attack speed makes it great for taking out flying bats and fast-moving dust sprites on floors 30-40, though it lacks the extra defense of the Steel Smallsword.
- **Slingshot (with copper ore ammo)**: 4-10 damage per hit, long range. You get the slingshot for free from a chest on mine level 7, and copper ore (which you’ll be mining constantly in the first 40 floors) works as free ammo. It’s perfect for taking out rock crabs from a distance before they can aggro, or hitting dust sprites that spawn in groups on floors 31-39. It’s not a great primary weapon, but it’s an excellent side tool to carry in your inventory.
- **Dagger**: 3-6 damage, very fast attack speed, +2 critical chance. Sold for 100g at the Adventurer’s Guild, the dagger is a great budget pick for players who prioritize speed over raw damage. Its fast attack speed lets you stun-lock most enemies, and the critical hit chance can land surprise 12-damage hits that one-shot slimes. It’s weaker than the Steel Smallsword or Wooden Club, but it’s a great option if you’re short on gold in your first week.

### B-Tier: Usable Only as Temporary Upgrades
These weapons are better than the starting rusty sword, but you’ll want to replace them as soon as you can get an S or A-Tier option.
- **Rusty Sword (starting weapon)**: 2-5 damage, average attack speed. It works for the first 10 floors, but you’ll struggle to kill even green slimes quickly once you hit floor 15. Replace it the second you find a better option.
- **Broken Trident**: 5-9 damage, average attack speed. A rare drop from fishing chests in the mountain lake, it’s a decent upgrade if you happen to get it while fishing for largemouth bass, but it’s not worth grinding fishing to find. It’s weaker than both S-Tier weapons and costs nothing if you get it as a random drop, but don’t go out of your way to farm for it.

## How to Get the Steel Smallsword (Best Free Early-Game Weapon)
The Steel Smallsword is the best stardew valley early game weapons for mines 1-40 for a reason: it’s completely free, requires no combat levels to get, and has better stats than most weapons you can buy for 500g or more in the early game. You can get it as early as Day 6 of Spring, before you even spend more than a few hours in the mines. Follow these step-by-step instructions to pick it up:
1. **Reach mine level 10**: You don’t need to kill every enemy on the way to level 10—just rush for ladders, use staircases if you have any from breaking rocks, and run past slimes and bats if they get too close. The rusty sword is strong enough to fight off any enemies that chase you, as long as you don’t get swarmed.
2. **Look for the standard wooden chest on level 10**: The chest spawns in a random spot on the floor, usually near the ladder to level 11 or in a corner of the mine. It’s not a hidden or secret chest—every save file has this chest on level 10, so you won’t have to grind for it to spawn.
3. **Open the chest to claim the Steel Smallsword**: It will be the only item in the chest, and you can equip it immediately.
4. **Optional: Buy a second one for backup if you lose it**: If you die in the mines and lose the Steel Smallsword, you can buy a replacement from the Adventurer’s Guild for 750g once you reach combat level 2. That’s still cheaper than most mid-tier early-game weapons, so it’s worth the cost if you lose your free copy.

Once you have the Steel Smallsword, you’ll notice a massive difference in mine runs. The +2 defense bonus reduces damage from green slimes from 5 damage to 3, and from rock crabs from 8 damage to 6, which means you’ll use half as many foraged berries or health potions per run. It’s strong enough to carry you all the way to floor 40, where you can start hunting for better weapons from frozen floors or upgrade to a steel sword from Clint the blacksmith once you have enough iron ore. ## Weapons to Avoid in Early Stardew Valley (Waste of Gold)
Not every weapon sold at the Adventurer’s Guild or found in early chests is worth your gold or inventory space. These weapons are overpriced, underpowered, or worse than free options you can get with minimal effort:
- **Wooden Blade**: 4-8 damage, sold for 200g at the Adventurer’s Guild. It’s only a tiny upgrade from the starting rusty sword, and costs 200g that you could spend on potato seeds, a backpack upgrade, or the much better 250g Wooden Club. The Wooden Club deals twice as much max damage for only 50g more, so there’s no reason to buy the Wooden Blade.
- **Falchion**: 6-12 damage, sold for 1,000g at the Adventurer’s Guild once you hit combat level 3. It has almost identical stats to the free Steel Smallsword, but costs 1,000g—an enormous sum in your first month, when you’re saving for tool upgrades and sprinklers. It’s only 1 extra max damage, which is not worth the cost when you can get the Steel Smallsword for free on mine level 10.
- **Neptune’s Glaive**: 10-20 damage, rare drop from fishing chests. It’s a powerful weapon, but it’s so rare that you can’t rely on getting it before you hit mine level 40. Grinding fishing for hours to try to get it will only delay your mine progression, and you can beat the first 40 floors easily with the Steel Smallsword instead.
- **Slingshot with stone ammo**: Stone ammo only deals 1-3 damage per hit, which is weaker than your starting rusty sword. If you’re going to use the slingshot, only use copper or iron ore as ammo—stone ammo is a waste of inventory space and doesn’t deal enough damage to be useful against even the weakest slimes.

Wasting gold on these weapons will set back your farm progression by days, if not a full week. Save your gold for tool upgrades, seeds, and the first backpack upgrade, and use free or cheap weapons from mine chests and the lower-priced Adventurer’s Guild options instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions
### What is the best weapon for mine floors 1-40 if I have no combat skills?
The Steel Smallsword is the best option for players with no combat skill points, as it’s free, has a +2 defense bonus that reduces incoming damage, and deals enough damage to one-shot most common enemies in the first 40 floors. If you can’t find it yet, the 250g Wooden Club from the Adventurer’s Guild is a great cheap alternative that requires no combat levels to buy or use.
### Can I beat mine levels 1-40 with the starting rusty sword?
Technically yes, but it will take you 2-3 times longer, you’ll use far more health items, and you’ll be more likely to die and lose half your gold and items. We recommend upgrading your weapon as soon as possible, ideally by mine level 10 when you can get the free Steel Smallsword.
### Do I need to level up my combat skill to get good early weapons?
No. The Steel Smallsword, Wooden Club, and slingshot are all available with zero combat skill levels. The only weapons that require combat levels are higher-priced guild options, which are worse than the free or low-cost options you can get without leveling combat.
### What’s the difference between swords, clubs, and daggers for early combat?
Swords (like the Steel Smallsword) are balanced, with average speed and damage plus a secondary block move that reduces incoming damage by 50% if you time it right. Clubs have higher damage and knockback but slower attack speed, making them great for crowd control. Daggers have very fast attack speed and high critical chance but low base damage, making them best for players who prefer fast, hit-and-run combat.

## Final Tips for Clearing Mine Levels 1-40 Fast
Even with the best early-game weapon, there are a few extra tips that will help you clear the first 40 floors in under a week of in-game time, so you can get iron ore for sprinklers and tool upgrades before Spring ends:
- **Bring food that grants a speed or attack buff**: Spicy eel (caught from fishing in the mines) gives a +1 speed and +1 attack buff that lasts 7 minutes, which makes running past enemies and killing them faster. Even foraged blackberries or daffodils work as emergency health items if you don’t have cooked food yet.
- **Only kill enemies that are in your way**: You don’t need to clear every enemy on a floor to find the ladder to the next level. Run past slimes and bats unless they drop useful items like slime or bat wings, which you can sell or use for crafting later.
- **Upgrade your pickaxe to copper as soon as possible**: A copper pickaxe breaks rocks in one hit instead of two, which lets you find ladders faster and get more copper ore for slingshot ammo or tool upgrades. You only need 5 copper ore and 2,000g to buy the upgrade from Clint.
- **Use bombs for large rock clusters**: You can buy bombs from the Adventurer’s Guild for 60g each, or find them in mine chests. Bombs clear 3x3 areas of rocks instantly, which makes finding ladders much faster, especially on floors with lots of stone and few enemies.
- **Always leave the mines before 2am**: If you pass out in the mines, you’ll lose half your gold and one random item, which could include your best weapon. Plan your runs so you have enough time to walk back to the farm before midnight, or use a totem of the farm if you have one to teleport back instantly.

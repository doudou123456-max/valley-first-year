## Top 3 Accessible Weapons Before Unlocking Mine Level 5 Elevator
All three of these weapons are obtainable within your first 5-7 days in Pelican Town, with no random loot drops, no copper tool requirements, and no need to spend gold. They all outperform the starter sword by a wide margin, and each has unique strengths for different playstyles.

### 1. Steel Smallsword
The Steel Smallsword is the most well-rounded early weapon in Stardew Valley, and it’s shockingly easy to get long before you step foot in the Mines. It has a perfect blend of damage, swing speed, and knockback that makes it ideal for new players still learning combat mechanics. If you’re looking for a one-size-fits-all weapon that works for every enemy in the first 10 mine levels, this is your best bet.

### 2. Wooden Club
For players who prefer high damage and wide swing arcs, the Wooden Club is a surprisingly powerful option. It’s slower than the swords on this list, but its area-of-effect (AoE) hit can take out multiple slimes at once, and its high knockback keeps enemies from getting close enough to hit you. It’s a great pick if you struggle with crowds of enemies or prefer to play more defensively.

### 3. Rusty Mace
The Rusty Mace is the most underrated early-game weapon, with a unique stun chance that can turn tough fights into one-sided victories. It’s slightly slower than the Steel Smallsword but deals more damage per hit, and the 10% stun chance means even rock crabs (the most annoying early-game enemy) can be taken out without taking damage. It’s perfect for players who want to minimize risk while clearing mine floors.

## How to Get Each Early Weapon (No RNG or Gold Required)
None of these weapons require you to farm rare drops, buy anything, or use upgraded tools. You can collect all three in your first week if you know where to look.

### How to Get the Steel Smallsword
The Steel Smallsword is given to you for free by Marlon, the leader of the Adventurer’s Guild, as soon as you enter the Mines for the very first time. There are no hoops to jump through, no quests to complete, and no need to fight anything to get it.
- Step 1: Reach the Mines entrance east of Pelican Town, located past the Carpenter’s Shop and the Blacksmith. You can get there as early as Day 5, when the earthquake clears the boulder blocking the path.
- Step 2: Walk up to the locked mine door. A cutscene will play where Marlon welcomes you to the Mines, explains the basic combat system, and hands you the Steel Smallsword for free.
- Step 3: Equip it immediately—it’s already better than the starter rusty sword you got on your first day.

You don’t need to fight a single enemy or have any tools to get this weapon, making it the most reliable option for every new player. If you want to practice with it before heading into the Mines, you can use it to kill slimes that spawn near the mine entrance on rainy days for extra experience.

### How to Get the Wooden Club
The Wooden Club is found in one of the earliest foraging spots in Pelican Town, no combat required. You can grab it on Day 1 if you know where to look, before you even unlock the Mines.
- Step 1: Head to the southeast corner of Pelican Town, near the river that runs past JojaMart and the community garden plot.
- Step 2: Look for a large, broken log pile tucked between the river and the fence that borders the railroad tracks (you won’t be able to access the railroad itself until later, but the log pile is on the public side of the fence).
- Step 3: Hit the log pile 5 times with your starter axe (no copper axe needed) to break it open. The Wooden Club will drop out, along with 3 pieces of hardwood.

This weapon is available before you even meet Marlon, so it’s a great pick if you want to practice combat on slimes that spawn around the map before you enter the Mines. Its wide swing arc also makes it useful for clearing weeds while you’re exploring, saving you inventory space for foraged items.

### How to Get the Rusty Mace
The Rusty Mace is a reward for completing one of the easiest early-game quests, with no combat or tool upgrades required. You can get it as early as Day 3 if you prioritize the quest.
- Step 1: Check the quest board outside Pierre’s General Store every morning. The “Slime Problem” quest will pop up randomly in your first 7 days, asking you to kill 10 slimes anywhere in Pelican Town.
- Step 2: Slimes spawn regularly in the forest west of your farm, near the Secret Woods entrance, and on rainy days near the beach and mine entrance. You can kill them with your starter sword, the Steel Smallsword, or even the Wooden Club if you already have it.
- Step 3: Once you’ve killed 10 slimes, return to the quest board to collect your reward: the Rusty Mace, plus 100 gold.

The slimes you need for this quest are all low-level, deal only 2 damage per hit, and are easy to kill even with the starter sword. You don’t need to enter the Mines to complete it, so this weapon is a great option if you want extra power before your first mine run.

### Best Weapon for Slimes
Slimes are the most common enemy in the first 10 mine levels, coming in green and blue variants that deal 2 and 4 damage per hit respectively. They move slowly, jump to attack, and often spawn in groups of 2-4.
- **Top pick: Wooden Club** – Its wide AoE swing can hit 2-3 slimes at once, clearing entire groups in 1-2 hits. ### Best Weapon for Bats
Flying bats spawn on Levels 5-10 of the Mines, deal 5 damage per hit, and fly in erratic patterns that make them hard to hit with slow weapons. They often attack from behind while you’re mining ore, so fast reaction time is key.
- **Top pick: Steel Smallsword** – Its fast swing speed makes it easy to track and hit moving bats, even when they’re flying around your head. You can take out most bats in one hit, and the medium knockback knocks them away before they can bite you.
- **Second pick: Rusty Mace** – Its medium swing speed is still fast enough to hit bats, and the stun effect will drop them out of the air immediately if it triggers.
- **Avoid if possible: Wooden Club** – Its very slow swing speed makes it almost impossible to hit moving bats. You’ll likely take 2-3 hits from a bat before you land a single swing with the club.

### Best Weapon for Rock Crabs
Rock Crabs are the toughest enemy in the first 10 mine levels, with 30 health, 8 damage per hit, and a defensive shell that makes them immune to damage when they curl up. They hide in plain sight as rocks, so you’ll often run right into them without warning.
- **Top pick: Rusty Mace** – The 10% stun chance is a game-changer for Rock Crabs. If you stun them right when they uncurl to attack, you can get 3-4 free hits in before they can curl back up or hit you. It only takes 3 hits to kill a Rock Crab with the Rusty Mace, so you’ll often take zero damage if the stun triggers.
- **Second pick: Steel Smallsword** – Its fast swing speed lets you get in quick hits right after the crab uncurls, before it can attack. You’ll need to time your swings carefully, but you can take them out without taking damage if you’re patient.
- **Avoid if possible: Wooden Club** – Its slow swing speed means you’ll only get one hit in before the crab curls back up, extending the fight for 10+ seconds. The high knockback can also push the crab away from you, giving it more time to reset its defense.

## Beginner Combat Tips to Survive the First 10 Mine Levels
Even with the best early-game weapon, the first 10 mine levels can be deadly if you don’t know what you’re doing. These tips will help you clear floors fast, avoid deaths, and unlock the Level 5 elevator in no time, even with no copper tools.

### Prioritize food for healing before you enter the Mines
You don’t need expensive cooking recipes to heal in the early game. Forageable berries, dandelions, leeks, and even the parsnips you plant on your first day will heal 10-30 health each. Bring at least 5-6 food items with you on every mine run, and keep them in your hotbar for quick access. If you’re low on foraged food, you can buy sashimi from the Stardrop Saloon for 80 gold each, which heals 40 health.

### Use the “hit and back up” strategy to avoid damage
Almost every enemy in the first 10 mine levels has a short attack range. The safest way to fight is to hit the enemy once, then immediately back up 1 tile to avoid their counterattack. Repeat until the enemy is dead. This strategy works especially well with the Steel Smallsword, as its fast swing speed lets you get in hits before enemies can react. For more combat strategy tips, check out our [Beginner Combat Guide for Stardew Valley](/guides/stardew-valley-beginner-combat-guide).

### Don’t mine every rock you see
Your first goal in the Mines is to reach Level 5 as fast as possible to unlock the elevator. Mining every ore and stone on a floor will waste time and use up your energy, leaving you with no stamina to fight enemies. Instead, look for ladders as soon as you enter a floor—they spawn under random rocks, so only break the rocks closest to you until you find the ladder. You can come back to mine ore later once you have the elevator unlocked.

### Clear flying bats first before mining
Bats are the biggest cause of unexpected deaths in the early Mines, as they attack from behind while you’re focused on breaking rocks. As soon as you enter a new floor, take 10 seconds to walk around and kill all the bats first before you start mining or looking for ladders. This will prevent you from getting surprised mid-swing.

### Know when to leave
If you’re down to 20 health or less and have no food left, don’t push for the next floor. The trip back to the clinic will cost you half your gold and 3 hours of time, which is far worse than leaving early to restock on food. You can use the stairs to exit the Mines at any time, and you won’t lose progress on the floors you’ve already cleared.
